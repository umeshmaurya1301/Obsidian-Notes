Before any service makes sense you need the map: what "cloud" actually buys you, how AWS physically lays out the world, and who is responsible when something breaks. Everything in notes 02–11 sits *inside* the structure described here.

> [!info] 🧭 Summary
> - **Cloud computing** = renting compute/storage/network on demand, billed per use. Swaps **CapEx** (buy servers) for **OpEx** (rent capacity).
> - **Service models**, by how much you manage: `IaaS` (EC2) → `CaaS` (ECS/EKS) → `PaaS` (Beanstalk) → `FaaS` (Lambda) → `SaaS` (WorkMail). Less management = less control.
> - **Region** = a geographic area (`ap-south-1` = Mumbai). **AZ** = one or more discrete data centres inside a Region — isolated power/cooling/network, linked by sub-millisecond private fibre.
> - **Multi-AZ = high availability** (survive a data-centre failure). **Multi-Region = disaster recovery + global latency**, and is far harder.
> - **Edge locations** (400+) are a third tier used by CloudFront/Route 53 — not for running your app.
> - Most services are **Region-scoped**. A few are **global**: `IAM`, `Route 53`, `CloudFront`, `Organizations`, and the S3 *bucket namespace*.
> - **Shared Responsibility Model**: AWS secures the cloud (hardware, hypervisor, sites); **you** secure what's *in* it (data, IAM, security groups, your OS/app patching).
> - You reach AWS three ways: **Console** (UI), **CLI/SDK** (scripting, your Spring Boot app), **IaC** (CloudFormation/CDK/Terraform).
>

## 1. What cloud computing actually is

On-demand delivery of IT resources over the internet, billed for what you use.

```text
Old world                      Cloud
-----------------------        -----------------------
Buy 20 servers up front        Rent 3 now, 20 at peak, 3 again at night
Guess capacity 3 yrs ahead     Change capacity in 60 seconds
Pay whether idle or not        Pay per second / per request
Own the hardware failure       AWS owns the hardware failure
```

Why it matters for a backend engineer:

- **Elasticity** — scale up for a sale, back down after. No over-provisioning.
- **Pay-as-you-go** — no idle hardware cost, no three-year procurement cycle.
- **Global reach** — deploy near users without building data centres.
- **Managed services** — RDS, ElastiCache and MSK mean you stop being a part-time DBA.

> [!warning] The flip side
> Pay-as-you-go cuts both ways. A forgotten `m7i.large`, an unattached EBS volume, an idle NAT Gateway, or a bucket full of old logs all bill silently. Tear down anything you spin up to learn.

## 2. Service models — who manages what

The models differ only in **where the line sits** between what you manage and what AWS manages.

| Model | You manage | AWS manages | AWS example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On-prem** | Everything | Nothing | — |
| **`IaaS`** | OS, runtime, app, data | Hardware, virtualization | **`EC2`**, `EBS`, `VPC` |
| **`CaaS`** | Container image, app, data | Host VMs, orchestration | **`ECS`**, **`EKS`**, `Fargate` |
| **`PaaS`** | App + data | OS, runtime, scaling | `Elastic Beanstalk`, `App Runner` |
| **`FaaS`** | Function code | Everything else | **`Lambda`** |
| **`SaaS`** | Your data/config only | Everything | `WorkMail`, `Chime` |

> [!example] The pizza mental model
> - **On-prem** — make the pizza at home; you buy everything.
> - **`IaaS`** — buy a frozen pizza, bake it in your own oven.
> - **`PaaS`** — order delivery; you supply the table and drinks (app + data).
> - **`SaaS`** — eat at the restaurant; you supply nothing but an appetite.

The trade-off is always the same: **control vs. operational burden**. `IaaS` gives you every knob *and* every 2 a.m. page. `FaaS` gives you almost no knobs and almost no pages.

## 3. Global infrastructure — Region → AZ → Edge

```text
AWS Global Infrastructure

  Region: ap-south-1 (Mumbai)
  |
  +-- AZ: ap-south-1a   [1+ data centres]
  +-- AZ: ap-south-1b   [1+ data centres]
  +-- AZ: ap-south-1c   [1+ data centres]
          ^
          |  isolated power / cooling / network
          |  private fibre between them, < 1 ms

  Edge Locations (400+, in far more cities than Regions)
     -> CloudFront (CDN), Route 53 (DNS), Global Accelerator
```

### Region

A **geographic area** containing multiple AZs, named `<area>-<direction>-<number>`:

| Region code | Location | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `us-east-1` | N. Virginia | Oldest, cheapest, gets services first, most outages |
| `ap-south-1` | Mumbai | Your default for Indian users / RBI localisation |
| `eu-west-1` | Ireland | Oldest European Region |

**How to choose a Region:**

1. **Latency** — closest to your users.
2. **Compliance / data residency** — RBI data-localisation means Indian payment data stays in `ap-south-1`.
3. **Service availability** — new services launch in `us-east-1` first.
4. **Cost** — the same instance is priced differently per Region.

### Availability Zone (AZ)

One or more discrete data centres with **independent power, cooling and networking** — far enough apart that one flood or fire won't take two down, close enough for **synchronous replication**.

This is the single most important reliability primitive in AWS:

- **RDS Multi-AZ** → synchronous standby in another AZ.
- **ASG across 3 AZs** → an AZ outage kills a third of your capacity, not your service.
- **S3** → replicates across ≥3 AZs automatically, for free.

### Edge location

Not for running your app — for **caching content and terminating connections close to users**. Used by `CloudFront`, `Route 53`, `Global Accelerator`, `WAF`.

> [!intuition] Region vs AZ — the interview one-liner
> "A Region is a *geographic* boundary — chosen for latency, compliance and cost. An AZ is a *failure* boundary inside a Region — chosen for high availability. I deploy across multiple AZs by default; multiple Regions only when DR or global latency demands it."

## 4. Regional vs global services

Getting this wrong is a classic slip.

| Scope | Services |
|---|---|
| **Global** | `IAM`, `Route 53`, `CloudFront`, `Organizations`, `WAF` (for CloudFront), S3 **bucket namespace** |
| **Regional** | `EC2`, `S3` data, `RDS`, `DynamoDB`, `Lambda`, `VPC`, `SQS`, `ECS/EKS` |
| **AZ-scoped** | `EBS` volumes, **subnets**, `NAT Gateway`, a single-AZ RDS instance |

Consequences you will actually hit:

- An **EBS volume cannot attach to an EC2 instance in another AZ.** Snapshot it, restore into the target AZ.
- A **subnet lives in exactly one AZ.** "Multi-AZ" therefore literally means "multiple subnets".
- **S3 bucket names are globally unique** across every AWS account on Earth — `user-images` was taken years ago.

## 5. Shared Responsibility Model

> [!info] The line
> **AWS is responsible for security *OF* the cloud. You are responsible for security *IN* the cloud.**

| AWS's side | Your side |
|---|---|
| Physical data-centre security | IAM users, roles, policies |
| Hardware, network, hypervisor | Security Groups & NACLs |
| Managed-service patching (RDS engine, Lambda runtime) | **Guest OS patching on EC2** |
| Global infrastructure availability | Encryption choices, key management |
| Service durability guarantees | Your data, your application code |

The line **moves with the service model** — this is the part interviewers probe:

```text
EC2      you patch the OS, you patch the app, you configure the SG
RDS      AWS patches the engine; you still own schema, users, SG, encryption
Lambda   AWS patches the runtime; you own the code and its IAM role
S3       AWS owns durability; YOU own whether the bucket is public
```

Every famous "AWS breach" headline has been a customer-side misconfiguration — a public S3 bucket or a leaked access key — not an AWS failure.

## 6. How you talk to AWS

| Method | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Console** | Learning, one-off inspection, debugging |
| **CLI** (`aws s3 ls`) | Scripting, CI/CD, quick ops |
| **SDK** (Java `software.amazon.awssdk`) | Your Spring Boot app calling S3/DynamoDB |
| **IaC** (CloudFormation, CDK, Terraform) | Anything that must be reproducible |

```java
// AWS SDK v2 — credentials resolved automatically from the
// instance / task / pod IAM role. Never hardcode keys.
S3Client s3 = S3Client.builder()
        .region(Region.AP_SOUTH_1)
        .build();
```

> [!gotcha] Credential resolution order
> The default provider chain looks in: Java system properties → environment variables → `~/.aws/credentials` → **container credentials (ECS/EKS)** → **EC2 instance metadata (IMDSv2)**. In production the last two should be doing the work. If your code contains an access key, that is the finding an interviewer is waiting for.

## 7. Cost model in one screen

You are billed on three axes: **compute time**, **storage volume**, **data transfer**.

- **Data transfer *in* is free. Data transfer *out* to the internet is not.** This dominates surprise bills.
- **Cross-AZ traffic costs money**, in both directions. A chatty service spread across AZs pays for the privilege.
- Guardrails: **Billing alarms**, **AWS Budgets**, **Cost Explorer**, and tagging every resource with `Environment` / `Owner` / `Service`.

## ⚠️ Gotchas & Limits

> [!warning]
> - **"Serverless" ≠ no servers.** Servers exist; AWS operates them invisibly. Lambda still has cold starts, memory limits and a 15-minute ceiling.
> - **Region ≠ AZ.** Multi-AZ protects against a data-centre failure. It does **not** protect against a Region-wide outage or a bad deploy.
> - **`us-east-1` is special** — global control planes for IAM, CloudFront and S3 bucket creation live there, which is why its outages feel global.
> - **AZ names are per-account.** Your `ap-south-1a` and mine may be different physical zones — AWS randomises the mapping to spread load. Use **AZ IDs** (`aps1-az1`) when it actually matters.
> - AWS never garbage-collects your forgotten resources. Deleting is on you.

## 🔑 Key Takeaways

- Cloud = rent, don't buy. The win is **elasticity + managed operations**, not raw cheapness.
- The service-model ladder is a **control vs. burden** dial: `IaaS → CaaS → PaaS → FaaS → SaaS`.
- **Region = geography** (latency/compliance/cost). **AZ = failure isolation** (HA). Multi-AZ is the default answer to "how do you make it highly available?"
- **EBS and subnets are AZ-scoped; IAM, Route 53 and CloudFront are global.** Everything else is regional.
- Shared Responsibility: AWS secures the cloud, **you secure what you put in it** — and the line shifts as you move up the service models.
