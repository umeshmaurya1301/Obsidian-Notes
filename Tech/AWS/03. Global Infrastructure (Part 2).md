# Lecture 3 – AWS Global Infrastructure (Part 2)

> [!info] Overview
> **Disaster Recovery (DR)** is your plan for keeping (or quickly restoring) service when a whole Region/AZ fails. Two metrics drive every DR decision: **RTO** (how fast you recover) and **RPO** (how much data you can lose). AWS frames DR as four strategies that trade **cost vs. RTO/RPO**: `Backup & Restore` → `Pilot Light` → `Warm Standby` → `Multi-Site Active/Active`.

---

## 🎯 Key Concepts

### RTO — Recovery Time Objective
- **Maximum acceptable downtime** — the time from disaster to full service recovery.
- "How long can we be *down*?"
- **Smaller RTO** = recover faster = need standby infrastructure ready to go = **more expensive**.

### RPO — Recovery Point Objective
- **Maximum acceptable data loss**, measured in *time* — the gap between your last good copy and the moment of disaster.
- "How much *data* can we afford to lose?"
- **Smaller RPO** = lose less data = need more frequent / continuous replication = **more expensive**.

> [!example] RTO vs RPO on a timeline
> ```
>        last backup            DISASTER            service back up
>  ──────────●────────────────────╳────────────────────●──────────▶ time
>            └──── RPO (data loss)─┘└──── RTO (downtime)─┘
> ```
> RPO looks *backward* (data lost since last copy). RTO looks *forward* (time to restore).

### When to choose what
- Tight **RTO** → keep infrastructure pre-provisioned and warm (Warm Standby / Active-Active).
- Tight **RPO** → use continuous / synchronous data replication, not periodic backups.
- Loose RTO **and** RPO (cost-sensitive, non-critical) → Backup & Restore is fine.
- The stricter (smaller) your targets, the higher the cost — pick the **cheapest strategy that still meets the business requirement**.

---

## 🛠️ The Four DR Strategies

Ordered from **cheapest / slowest recovery** → **costliest / fastest recovery**:

### 1. Backup & Restore
- Back up data (snapshots, `S3`) — often to another Region — and **restore on demand** after a disaster: provision infra, then load data.
- **Cost:** lowest. **RTO/RPO:** highest (hours). Nothing runs in the DR Region until needed (cold).

### 2. Pilot Light
- A **minimal core** always running in the DR Region — typically the **database replicating continuously** — while app/compute servers stay off or tiny.
- On disaster, you **start and scale up** the rest around the always-on core.
- Like a gas heater's pilot flame: small but always lit, ready to ignite the full system.
- **Cost:** low–medium. **RTO/RPO:** much better than Backup & Restore.

### 3. Warm Standby (Active / Passive)
- A **scaled-down but fully functional** copy of production always running in the DR Region.
- On disaster, **scale it up to full capacity** and shift traffic — no cold start.
- **Cost:** medium–high. **RTO/RPO:** low (minutes).

### 4. Multi-Site Active / Active
- **Full production runs in multiple Regions simultaneously**, all serving live traffic.
- A Region failure just routes users to the survivors — near-**zero RTO and RPO**.
- **Cost:** highest. Requires multi-Region data replication and conflict handling.

| Strategy | RTO | RPO | Cost | DR Region state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backup & Restore | Hours | Hours | $ | Cold (data only) |
| Pilot Light | 10s of min | Minutes | $$ | Core/DB on, rest off |
| Warm Standby | Minutes | Seconds–min | $$$ | Scaled-down live copy |
| Active / Active | ~0 | ~0 | $$$$ | Full live copy |

### How the database is resolved (replication)
- **Backup & Restore:** periodic snapshots/backups copied to DR — restore on failover.
- **Pilot Light / Warm Standby:** **continuous replication** (e.g. `RDS` read replica / standby kept in sync).
- **Active / Active:** **multi-Region, often bidirectional** replication — e.g. `DynamoDB Global Tables`, `Aurora Global Database`. Watch for write conflicts.

---

## ⚠️ Gotchas & Limits
> [!warning]
> - **RTO ≠ RPO.** RTO = downtime tolerance (forward); RPO = data-loss tolerance (backward). Interviewers love confirming you don't mix these up.
> - **Smaller RTO/RPO costs more** — don't over-engineer; match the strategy to the business need.
> - **Pilot Light vs Warm Standby:** Pilot Light has the *core* running and you build the rest up; Warm Standby has the *whole stack* running, just smaller. The line is how much is already live.
> - **Active/Active is hardest** — multi-Region writes mean replication lag and **conflict resolution**, not just extra cost.
> - Test failover regularly — an untested DR plan is not a DR plan.

---

## 🔑 Key Takeaways
- **RTO** = how long you're down; **RPO** = how much data you lose. Both smaller ⇒ more expensive.
- Four strategies, cheap→pricey: **Backup & Restore → Pilot Light → Warm Standby → Active/Active**.
- Recovery speed (RTO) improves as you keep more infrastructure pre-running; data loss (RPO) improves with more continuous replication.
- Pick the **cheapest strategy meeting your RTO/RPO targets** — DR is a cost-vs-resilience trade-off.

---

## 🎤 Interview Tips
- In system design, when asked about resilience, state your **RTO/RPO targets first**, then justify a DR strategy from them — that shows requirements-driven thinking.
- Name-drop the right AWS primitives: cross-Region `S3`/snapshot copy (Backup & Restore), `RDS` read replicas (Pilot Light/Warm Standby), `Aurora Global Database` / `DynamoDB Global Tables` + `Route 53` failover routing (Active/Active).
- Multi-AZ vs Multi-Region: **Multi-AZ** = high availability within a Region (handles AZ failure); **Multi-Region DR** = surviving a whole-Region outage. Don't conflate them.
